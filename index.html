Project 01 · Featured

Smart Personal Finance Tracker

Treated a Kaggle personal finance dataset the same way I'd treat a real client problem — structured the question first, defined 12 KPIs before touching the data, then validated each one. The central question: why does someone earning $883K over 5 years end up in financial deficit 67% of the time?

Found that 52.6% of income was going to discretionary categories — nearly double the 50/30/20 recommended 30%. Income wasn't the problem; the trajectory was actually improving. Three targeted interventions projected to flip -$3,250/month to +$1,200–$1,800/month within 6 months.

Before any analysis, caught a silent data quality bug: all 146 Salary transactions were classified as Expense — a $149,054 understatement that would have corrupted every downstream KPI.

Project 02

Rossmann Drug Store Sales Forecasting

Forecasted 6-week sales for 1,115 German drug stores across August–September 2015. Treated as a real operational problem: outputs designed for store managers to set staffing and place stock orders, not just a model benchmark.

Key architectural decision: K-Means clustering before model training. Training one model across all stores forces it to average fundamentally different store profiles. Clustering first, then training within each cluster, reduced average RMSPE by 29.7%.

Analytical Philosophy

How I Approach Every Engagement

A set of working principles I've carried from engineering into analytics — the ones that actually change the quality of the output.

① Define the question first. "Explore the data" is a task. "Why is this person in deficit 67% of the time?" is a question. One gives you charts, the other gives you answers.
② Pre-commit to metrics. KPIs defined before touching the data, not after. Choosing them afterwards is selection bias dressed up as analysis.
③ Check the data before trusting it. The worst errors don't crash your code — they silently corrupt your conclusions.
④ Tell clients what the data says. Not what they hoped to hear. A founder who thinks churn is a pricing problem needs someone willing to show it isn't.
⑤ The right tool is the one that gets used. Looker Studio over Power BI if the client lives in Google Workspace. Excel over Python if the output needs to be maintained by a non-coder.
